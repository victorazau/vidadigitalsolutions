## Why Your CRM Choice Matters So Much for a Law Firm

US law firms pay, on average, between $50 and $300 per click on Google Ads in practice areas like immigration law, family law, and personal injury. With CPCs that high, leaving a lead without a response for more than 15 minutes can cost hundreds of dollars — not in wasted ad spend, but in a potential client who signed with a competitor.

The problem is that most legal tools were built to **organize work after the client has already signed**. They manage cases, track billable hours, and generate invoices. They do that exceptionally well. But the top-of-funnel stage — the moment a lead fills out a form, calls the office, or sends a message at 10 PM asking about a consultation — is left completely unaddressed.

That's exactly the gap where your CRM decision becomes a strategic one.

## Clio: The Gold Standard for Legal Practice Management

Clio is, without question, the most complete legal CRM in the North American market. Founded in 2008 and used by more than 150,000 attorneys across the US and Canada, it was built from the ground up with compliance, confidentiality, and the operational needs of a law firm in mind.

**Clio's strengths:**
- Time tracking integrated directly with billing — click to start the timer, bill automatically
- Full matter management with documents, tasks, and court deadlines
- Clio Grow (separate paid product) for intake and scheduling
- Integrations with QuickBooks, Outlook, Google Workspace, and dozens of legal tools
- Native trust accounting — essential for compliance with state bar associations

**Clio's limitations:**
- Pricing scales quickly — $119/user/month on the full plan means $1,190/month for 10 attorneys
- Clio Grow (for lead capture) is sold separately and still falls well short of GoHighLevel's automation capabilities
- No native SMS marketing, no funnels, no landing pages
- Limited configurability for firm-specific workflows

Clio makes the most sense for firms with an established client base, where the primary challenge is **organizing and billing existing work** rather than generating more leads.

## MyCase: Balancing Case Management and Client Communication

MyCase is often described as "the more accessible Clio." It offers case management, a client portal, e-signatures, and centralized communication at a price point that fits better in smaller firms' budgets.

**MyCase's strengths:**
- Intuitive client portal — clients can view case status, sign documents, and message their attorney in one place
- More affordable than Clio for small teams
- Basic intake with forms and e-signatures
- Modern, easy-to-learn interface

**MyCase's limitations:**
- Time tracking and billing less robust than Clio
- No follow-up automation — no automatic SMS or email for leads who haven't responded
- Very basic lead pipeline
- Smaller integration ecosystem

MyCase is a solid choice for 2–5 attorney firms that want to organize case management without a large investment, but who **don't rely heavily on digital marketing to attract clients**.
