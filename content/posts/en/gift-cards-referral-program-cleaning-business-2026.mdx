## How to Build a Referral Program That Runs on Autopilot

An effective referral program for a cleaning business has three components:

1. **A clear incentive** — for both the person referring and the person being referred
2. **A unique tracking link** for each client
3. **Automated communication** at every step

### The model that works best: the double-sided discount

The simplest and most proven structure is:

- The referring client: earns **$25 in credit** toward their next cleaning
- The referred client: gets **$25 off** their first cleaning

This model works because it removes the hesitation from the person doing the referring ("will I seem pushy or self-serving?") — the new client benefits too. And the total acquisition cost ($50 in credit) is far lower than the average cost of a qualified lead through Google Ads in most US metro areas, which can run anywhere from $80 to $150 per lead.

### The automated referral flow, step by step

1. Client books their first cleaning
2. After the service: automatic SMS with their personalized referral link
3. Client shares the link with a friend
4. Friend fills out the form and books → enters the CRM tagged as "referral"
5. CRM notifies you and automatically applies the discount
6. After the friend's cleaning: SMS to the original client with their $25 credit
7. CRM logs the credit and reminds you at the next invoice

## How to Promote the Program to Your Current Clients

Building the system is only half the battle — your clients need to know it exists. Here's how to get the word out across every channel:

### SMS (highest open rate — 98%)
Send after every completed cleaning:
> *"Hi [Name]! Thanks for today 🙏 We have a referral program — you and your friend both get $25 off. Here's your personal link: [link]. Let me know if you have any questions!"*

### Email (automated 24 hours after service)
A clean, simple email explaining the program, how it works, and a CTA button to copy their referral link.

### Physical card (optional, but surprisingly effective)
A laminated business card left at the home after each cleaning with the program's QR code. Cost: under $0.50 per card. Many clients keep it in a drawer and pass it along to friends months later.

## Common Mistakes That Kill the Program Before It Starts

**1. The reward is too small:** A $10 discount doesn't motivate anyone to refer. Start with at least $25.

**2. The process is too complicated:** If a client has to call, send an email, and wait for confirmation, they'll give up. The link needs to work automatically.

**3. No tracking in place:** If you don't know where your referrals are coming from, you don't know what's working. Use CRM tags for every referral source.

**4. Mentioning it once and moving on:** Remind your clients about the program at least every 60 days. Set up a recurring automated email to do this for you.

**5. No clear terms:** Define whether credits expire, whether they can stack, and whether they apply only to regular services. Transparency prevents disputes.

**Q: Are there specific US regulations I need to know about for cleaning gift cards?**
**A:** Yes. Under federal law (the Credit CARD Act of 2009), gift cards cannot expire in less than 5 years, and inactivity fees can only be charged after 12 consecutive months of no use. States like California have even stricter rules. Consult a local accountant or attorney before launching — and never set an expiration date under one year on your gift cards.
