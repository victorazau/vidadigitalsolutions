## How Review Automation Actually Works

Review request automation works by identifying a trigger — such as a case closing, a consultation payment being processed, or documents being sent — and automatically firing off an SMS or email asking the client to share their experience on Google.

In GoHighLevel, that workflow looks like this:

1. **Trigger:** Case status changes to "Closed" in the firm's pipeline
2. **Delay:** The system waits 24 to 48 hours (giving the client time to process the outcome)
3. **Message:** A personalized SMS with the client's name and a direct link to your Google review page
4. **Follow-up:** If no review is posted within 5 days, a polite follow-up email goes out automatically
5. **Monitoring:** The system consolidates all reviews into a centralized dashboard

No attorney or paralegal needs to remember to send anything. The process runs on autopilot, every single day, for every single client.

Automation doesn't replace the human response to a negative review — but it can detect the moment a negative review is posted and alert the responsible partner within minutes, ensuring a response goes up within the first few hours. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is it ethical for attorneys to ask clients for reviews?**
**A:** Yes — as long as the request is genuine and unconditional. You cannot offer discounts, gifts, or any incentive in exchange for a positive review. Simply asking a client to share their honest experience, without influencing the content of the review, is permitted under Google's guidelines and complies with ABA Model Rules on attorney advertising. Automation just makes that ask consistent and well-timed.

**Q: How many Google reviews does a law firm need to rank on Google Maps?**
**A:** There's no hard minimum, but in practice, firms with 30 or more recent reviews (within the last 12 months) and an average rating above 4.5 stars have a significant advantage in local Google rankings. The algorithm weighs volume, recency, and relevance. The more consistent your flow of new reviews, the better your visibility.

**Q: What should we do when a client leaves a negative review?**
**A:** Respond within 24 hours — professionally and empathetically — without disclosing any details of the case (preserving attorney-client confidentiality). Thank the reviewer for their feedback, acknowledge their experience, and offer a direct channel to resolve the issue offline. Never argue or invalidate the review publicly. A well-crafted response to a negative review frequently reassures other potential clients that your firm is trustworthy and accountable.
